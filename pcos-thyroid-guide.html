01 / THE BASICS

Three conditions. Different needs.

PCOS affects reproductive and metabolic health. Thyroid disorders alter hormone production and can affect energy, heart rate and menstrual cycles. Symptoms overlap, so blood tests and clinical evaluation matter.

Hormonal & metabolic

PCOS

Polycystic ovary syndrome may involve irregular ovulation, higher androgen activity and insulin resistance. It can occur at any body weight.

Common signs
  • Irregular or absent periods
  • Acne, unwanted facial hair or scalp hair thinning
  • Fertility difficulties
  • Metabolic changes, sometimes weight gain

Management: sustainable activity, nutrition, sleep, metabolic screening and clinician-directed treatment.

Underactive thyroid

Hypothyroidism

The thyroid produces too little hormone; Hashimoto's autoimmune thyroiditis is a common cause. Treatment often requires levothyroxine.

Common signs
  • Fatigue and reduced exercise tolerance
  • Cold sensitivity and constipation
  • Dry skin, muscle aches
  • Weight and menstrual changes

Management: appropriate medication, follow-up testing, gradual conditioning and adequate nutrition.

Overactive thyroid

Hyperthyroidism

Excess thyroid hormone can overwork the heart and muscles. Causes include Graves' disease and overactive thyroid nodules.

Common signs
  • Fast heartbeat or palpitations
  • Heat intolerance and tremor
  • Unexplained weight loss
  • Anxiety, poor sleep and weakness

Management: medical stabilisation first, followed by a carefully graded return to exercise.

Can lifestyle cure these conditions? No single workout, diet or supplement cures PCOS or autoimmune thyroid disease. Many symptoms and health risks can improve with appropriate medical treatment and consistent lifestyle support.

02 / EVERYDAY FOUNDATIONS

Small habits. Meaningful progress.

Build these habits

  • Consistent sleep, typically 7–9 hours per night
  • Walk or move regularly; break up prolonged sitting
  • Strength train with good technique and sensible recovery
  • Include protein, vegetables, whole fruit and fibre-rich carbohydrates
  • Track energy, menstrual changes and exercise tolerance
  • Follow prescribed medication and scheduled medical reviews

Limit or avoid

  • Crash dieting and unplanned fasting when it undermines intake or adherence
  • Excess sugary drinks and frequent highly processed, fried foods
  • Smoking and heavy alcohol use
  • High-dose iodine, kelp or selenium supplements without medical advice
  • Training through palpitations, dizziness, chest pain or marked weakness
  • Assuming fatigue or missing periods are simply a fitness issue

Strength with a sustainable rhythm.

Weeks 1–4

Foundation

Start with 2 strength days; 1–2 sets of 10–15 repetitions per exercise. Keep effort around 5–6/10 and build towards 150 minutes of total moderate activity per week as tolerated.

Weeks 5–8

Build

Increase to 2–3 strength days; 2–3 sets of 8–12 reps with controlled technique. Add walking duration gradually. Avoid rushing to high-intensity intervals.

Weeks 9–12

Progress

Work towards 3 strength days with 2–3 sets, comfortably challenging loads and regular aerobic activity. Increase load only when prescribed reps feel controlled and recovery is good.

Workout adjustments: Stable hypothyroidism may need slower progression on fatigued days. For active or uncontrolled hyperthyroidism, avoid HIIT, heavy lifting and vigorous endurance work until the treating clinician confirms stability. Pregnancy requires a separate exercise assessment and plan.

04 / NOURISHMENT FIRST

Real food. No extreme restrictions.

There is no single scientifically proven 'PCOS diet'. A balanced pattern with adequate protein, vegetables, fibre and portion-aware carbohydrates is easier to sustain. Assamese staples can absolutely fit.

Build your plate

½ vegetables¼ protein¼ rice / roti / grains

Examples: dal, masor tenga or other fish curry, eggs, chicken, paneer/tofu, leafy xaak, seasonal vegetables, fruit, curd, rice and roti. Portions vary with your individual requirements.

Important food truths

Rice, fruit, dairy, soy and cooked cabbage/cauliflower do not need blanket bans. Gluten-free eating is not necessary unless there is a specific diagnosis. Avoid unprescribed thyroid 'boosters' and high-dose iodine.

If taking levothyroxine, follow your clinician's timing instructions; usually on an empty stomach, and separate iron/calcium supplements by about 4 hours.

05 / TRAIN SAFELY

Know when to seek care.

Pause exercise and seek urgent help

  • Chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness, or an irregular/racing heartbeat with symptoms
  • Severe fever, confusion or agitation with possible untreated hyperthyroidism
  • New severe weakness, altered awareness or dangerously low body temperature

Arrange medical review

  • New or worsening irregular menstrual cycles, heavy bleeding or missed periods
  • Persistent unexplained fatigue, tremor, heat intolerance or rapid weight change
  • Pregnancy or plans to conceive while managing thyroid disease
  • Fever or severe sore throat while taking antithyroid medicines: contact the prescriber urgently

A physician may assess TSH/FT4, and PCOS-related metabolic risks such as glucose tolerance, blood pressure and lipids. Coaches should never interpret lab results as a diagnosis or adjust medications.
